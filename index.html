Adobe Sign · US-based agency client

From a two-day blocker to a form that finally worked

~30% shorter setup
+

The problem

A US-based agency client was stuck for two days trying to set up a tax form. They wanted one option to grey out the others once selected. Emails kept going back and forth, and the form still didn't work.

What I did

I stopped the email thread and moved to a screen-share, because this was easier to see than describe. I found what was wrong in the form setup and fixed it live with the client watching, then flagged the same gap to engineering, since it was a recurring problem and not a one-off.

What changed

Before: two days stuck, no working form. After: the client completed the form correctly on the first try, with setup time down about 30%.

What I'd do differently

Write a short how-to guide for this exact setup, so the next customer can solve it alone.

Moving from email to a live screen-share unblocked the client, and the product gap behind it reached the team that could fix it for everyone.

Adobe Sign · Advanced plan, transaction usage

When testing started costing the customer real money

Test ≠ Live
+

The problem

Customers on the Advanced plan pay per production transaction. Testing their workflows used up those same paid transactions, so every test cost real money — and people got afraid to try things.

What I did

I spotted the cause: testing and real sending happened in the same place. I worked with engineering to set up a separate developer and testing account, watermarked "Test Purpose," with admin-controlled access.

What changed

Before: every test used a paid transaction. After: testing has its own clearly marked account, and paid transactions are saved for real documents.

What I'd do differently

Track how many test sends the new account absorbs per workflow, so the saving is a real number, not just a benefit.

Testing no longer eats into paid usage, so customers can improve their workflows safely.

Adobe Sign · Enterprise migration, US healthcare company

The license was assigned. So why couldn't the user log in?

3 → 1 friction points
+

The problem

A leading US healthcare company moved from Adobe Acrobat to Adobe Sign's enterprise solution for HIPAA-compliant e-signatures, adding extra identity checks. After the upgrade, users still struggled to get in — licensing, activation, and the web-based experience were easy to mix up, and the added verification made it harder to tell which had failed.

What I did

I mapped the whole journey, not just the ticket: from the admin assigning licenses to the user's invitation, activation, and multi-step verification.

What changed

Before: the license was assigned, yet users couldn't get in, and nobody could tell which step had failed. After: one clear onboarding sequence anyone on the team could follow.

What I'd do differently

Turn the sequence into a one-page guide with recreated screenshots, no real patient or customer data, for admins to hand out on upgrade day.

An enterprise HIPAA upgrade only works when people can actually log in, so I mapped licensing, activation, and verification into one simple path.

Personal automation project · Make.com

From spreadsheet to automated household meal planning

~60% less manual effort
+

The problem

Every month, planning the household menu meant creating calendar events one by one and adding the right people to each. Slow and repetitive.

What I did

I kept the menu in Google Sheets and built a Make.com workflow that reads it and creates events automatically — four steps: Google Sheets → Iterator → Array Aggregator → Google Calendar.

What changed

Before: every event and guest list added by hand. After: events are created automatically with the right attendees — manual effort down about 60%, by my own estimate.

What I'd do differently

Time the old and new process side by side, so the 60% is measured, not estimated.

A monthly chore now runs from one sheet, and the calendar fills itself.

Personal automation project · Notion + Zapier

From manual follow-ups to automated gym fee reminders

Manual → Automatic
+

The problem

Gym membership payments were tracked by hand. Every due date had to be checked, and every reminder sent one by one — repetitive work that came back every month.

What I did

I built a Notion membership tracker with a due date and payment status per member, and connected it to Zapier so upcoming payments trigger reminders on their own.

What changed

Before: someone checked dates and sent reminders manually. After: a structured tracker and automatic workflow do the checking. This is a prototype tested with 3 sample members, so no time saving is claimed yet.

What I'd do differently

Measure how many minutes one manual reminder really takes, and extend the same tracker beyond payments — a gym's real risk isn't late fees, it's a member who keeps paying but quietly stops showing up. The same fields could trigger a re-engagement email instead, which protects far more revenue than fees ever did.

Chasing payments now starts from the tracker, not from someone's memory — and the same trigger point could keep members engaged, not just paid up.

NortonLifeLock · Customer support and upselling

Turning customer support into trust-led selling

17 of 52 (≈33%)
+

The problem

Support agents had to fix technical problems and meet upsell targets at the same time. Pushing a sale too early risked losing a customer's trust.

What I did

I changed the sequence to "issue first, sale second": build rapport, explain the issue simply, fully resolve it, and only then recommend something, when there was a genuine need.

What changed

Before: resolution and selling competed in the same conversation. After: a clear sequence — listen, resolve, understand need, recommend. On one measured day, 17 of 52 chats, about a third, ended with the customer retained or upsold. I was recognised with Employee of the Quarter multiple times.

What I'd do differently

Track this across more than one day, so 33% becomes a trend and not a single sample, and record satisfaction afterward too.

Solving the problem first made the recommendation feel like help, not a pitch — and on one measured day, that turned into a sale or a retained customer about a third of the time.
